# Customer Onboarding Playbook

A step-by-step process for taking a new customer from "signed" to "fully
live" - written for whoever owns onboarding, no product or engineering
background required. Companion to the Live Demo Playbook, which covers the
sales call before the deal closes; this one starts the day after.

Target: a customer is confident, self-sufficient, and getting value within
30 days of signing.

## Before you reach out

- Confirm the signed plan, seat count, and billing start date.
- Confirm whether they need HIPAA mode enabled and a business associate
  agreement signed - flag this immediately if so, it gates other work.
- Note anything promised during the sales process (a data migration, a
  custom field set, a specific integration) so onboarding doesn't miss it.
- Every new organization is created with sample leads, referrals, contacts,
  and a starter marketing campaign already loaded, so the account never
  opens empty. Plan to walk the customer through replacing that sample data
  with their own in week 1 - see the checklist below.

## Day 0: Welcome and kickoff call (30 min)

**Goal:** the customer leaves this call knowing exactly what happens next
and has already taken one real action in the account.

1. Send a welcome email same day as signing, with login instructions and
   the kickoff call booked - don't let momentum cool between contract and
   first touch.
2. On the call, confirm who on their side owns setup (usually the person
   who bought it isn't the person who'll configure it - get the actual
   admin's name).
3. Walk through the sample data already in their account and explain it's
   there to show the shape of things, not to keep - "this is what a lead
   looks like, we'll replace these three with your real ones this week."
4. Set expectations for the next 30 days: a short list of milestones (team
   invited, real data in, first week live, 30-day check-in), not a wall of
   detail.
5. End with one concrete task before the call ends: invite one teammate, or
   add one real lead. Getting a second click in during the call beats any
   number of "I'll do it later" promises.

## Week 1: Core setup

This is the highest-dropout week - if the account isn't configured to match
how the customer actually works by day 7, adoption stalls. Prioritize in
this order:

1. **Team invited.** Get every day-one user invited and logged in, with the
   right role assigned (owner, admission manager, or liaison). Don't wait
   for "the full team" - invite whoever needs access this week and add
   stragglers later.
2. **Fields match their process.** Review the default lead and referral
   fields against how they actually track things. Add, rename, or remove
   fields so the board reflects their process, not a generic template.
   Remind them this takes seconds, no ticket required.
3. **Pipeline stages match their language.** Update the default stage names
   (New, Contacted, Qualified, Proposal, Won, Lost) to whatever they
   actually call each stage internally.
4. **Real data replaces sample data.** Either bulk-import their existing
   spreadsheet through the import screen, or delete the sample leads and
   referrals once real ones are flowing in. Don't leave both sitting
   side by side past week 1 - it muddies their first reports.
5. **Territory/county setup**, if they route work geographically - add
   their real counties or regions and assign the right person to each.

## Week 2: Team enablement and daily workflow

1. Run one short training session (15-20 min, screen share) for the staff
   who'll use the board daily - inline editing, adding a lead, moving a
   stage, using filters. Keep it hands-on, not a feature tour.
2. Confirm calendar is connected for anyone who needs it (Google or
   Outlook), and that their public booking page reflects real availability
   if they plan to use it.
3. If they log mileage or expenses, get one real entry into each log type
   during this week so the habit starts early, not at month-end when
   totals are due.
4. Check in async (not another meeting) - a short message asking "anything
   confusing so far?" catches friction before it becomes a support ticket.

## Week 3: Marketing and reporting, if applicable

Only run this section for customers actually planning to use these
features - don't push it on everyone by default.

1. If they'll send email campaigns, they need to connect their own mailbox
   (Gmail or Outlook) or verify a sending domain before anything can
   actually send - flag this early since domain verification takes time.
2. Review the intake form and landing page that come pre-loaded, and swap
   the sample copy for their real messaging if they intend to publish
   either one.
3. Walk through the analytics dashboards together once there's a real
   week of data behind them - this is more convincing with their numbers
   than a demo's sample numbers ever was.

## Week 4: 30-day check-in

1. Send a short survey or hold a 15-minute call: are they hitting the
   milestones set on day 0? What's still confusing?
2. Pull real usage: are the day-one invited users actually logging in?
   A customer with zero weekly logins after 30 days is a churn risk,
   not a slow starter - escalate internally, don't wait for a renewal
   conversation to surface it.
3. Confirm billing matches actual seat count - team size often changes
   in the first month.
4. Formally close out onboarding: tell them who to contact going forward
   (support portal, not you personally) and mark the account as live.

## Roles and handoff

- **Onboarding owner:** runs the kickoff, checks in through day 30, and is
  the single point of contact until handoff.
- **Customer's admin:** the person who actually configures fields, invites
  the team, and owns day-to-day account decisions - identify this person
  on the kickoff call, not after.
- **Support handoff:** once 30-day check-in is complete, hand the customer
  to the support portal for ongoing questions. Tell them this explicitly -
  a customer who keeps emailing their onboarding contact after handoff is a
  sign the handoff wasn't communicated clearly.

## Signs onboarding is off track

- No login from the admin within 48 hours of the welcome email.
- Sample data still untouched by the end of week 1.
- Nobody invited beyond the original signer by week 2.
- Silence after a check-in message - a quiet account is not a fine account.

Any one of these is worth a direct phone call, not another email.

## Exit criteria - when onboarding is done

- Real data has replaced the sample leads and referrals.
- Every day-one user has logged in at least once.
- Pipeline stages and fields match how the customer actually works.
- The 30-day check-in happened and usage looks healthy.
- The customer knows where to go for help next (support portal, not you).

Once all five are true, close onboarding and move the account to standard
support.
