# Live Demo Playbook

A script for running a great sales demo when a prospect books time with us.
Written for anyone on the team to pick up and run confidently - no product
or engineering background required. Built around a 30-minute call, with notes
for stretching to 45.

## Before the call

- Confirm the prospect's industry and biggest pain point from the booking
  form or discovery notes. Load the demo account with sample data that looks
  like their world (their terminology for "lead," their typical stages).
- Never open on an empty account. An empty screen makes us look unfinished.
  Seed leads, referrals, contacts, and a few completed tasks ahead of time.
- Have two browser windows signed in as two different roles (an owner
  account and a staff account) so the permissions moment in Part 3 is instant,
  not something you set up live.
- Confirm the call length with the prospect at the top: 30 minutes tight, or
  do they have 45. Adjust using the "if short on time" notes below.
- The full walkthrough below runs about 37 minutes, so a 30-minute call means
  choosing what to drop before you dial, not improvising mid-demo. The
  standard 30-minute cut is: drop Part 7 (marketing) entirely, trim Part 9 to
  building one chart, and skip Part 10 unless they're regulated. Decide this
  in advance based on the pain point they gave you.
- Know one competitor or method they're likely switching from (spreadsheet,
  a generic CRM, paper). Tailor the story to what you're replacing.

## Opening: discovery before the screen share (5 min)

Do not share your screen yet. Ask, and actually listen:

1. "Walk me through how you track leads and referrals today."
2. "What's the most frustrating part of that process?"
3. "If this call goes well, what does success look like three months from
   now?"

Repeat their pain point back in their words before moving on: "So today
things get lost between the spreadsheet and email, and you don't have a
single place your team can see status - that's exactly what I want to show
you solving." This one sentence is what earns you the next 25 minutes.

## The walkthrough

Share your screen. Lead with outcomes, not setup screens - skip the sign-up
and login flow entirely, or mention it in one sentence ("your team logs in
with email, or a passkey if they'd rather skip passwords - takes 30 seconds
either way").

### 1. One home base for every lead and referral (6 min)

**Show:** the pipeline board, already full of realistic sample records.

**Talk track:** "This is the whole pipeline at a glance - every lead, what
stage it's in, who owns it. No more piecing it together across a spreadsheet
and three inboxes."

- Create one lead live, drag it or update its stage.
- Edit a field inline (status, owner, a date) and point out there's no save
  button - it's already saved.
- Add a brand-new custom field on the spot (a dropdown or a checkbox) and
  say plainly: "If you need to track something we don't have a field for
  yet, you add it yourself, right now, no waiting on us to build it."

**Why it matters to them:** speed and independence. They're not filing a
ticket and waiting a week every time their process changes.

### 2. Nothing gets lost, and you can always see what happened (3 min)

**Show:** the history/timeline view on the lead you just edited.

**Talk track:** "Every change is logged - who changed what, and when. If
someone fat-fingers a status update, you can see exactly what it was before
and put it back with one click."

**Why it matters to them:** accountability without babysitting the team, and
a real audit trail if they're ever asked "who changed this."

### 3. Your team, without the chaos (4 min)

**Show:** switch between the owner window and the staff window side by side.

**Talk track:** "Not everyone should see everything. Owners see billing and
full history. Your frontline staff see and update their own leads, nothing
they shouldn't. You control that per person, and it takes one click to
invite someone new."

**Why it matters to them:** this is the moment that lands hardest for
healthcare, finance, or any regulated prospect - security and least-privilege
access, explained in one visual.

### 4. Contacts that don't turn into duplicates (2 min)

**Show:** create a contact or company that's a near-match to one that
already exists.

**Talk track:** "We catch this before it becomes two records for the same
person - one of the most common ways CRMs turn into a mess over time."

### 5. Bring your existing list in painlessly (2 min)

**Show:** the import screen. Drop in an Excel file - ideally one with the
column names deliberately worded differently from ours, and one column we
have no field for at all.

**Talk track:** "Drop in your existing Excel file or CSV - if the workbook
has several tabs, you pick the one you want. We read your column names, not
ours: your sheet says 'Facility Type,' our field is called 'Type of
Facility,' and it matches those up for you. Anything it isn't sure about it
asks you about instead of guessing - and if you've got a column we don't
have a field for, you create it right here in one click. Nothing is written
until you say go."

**Why it matters to them:** removes the single biggest fear prospects have
about switching tools - "what happens to everything we already have." The
detail that lands is that they don't have to reformat their spreadsheet to
match us, and nothing silently disappears: any column that isn't imported is
listed back to them by name.

*Prospects who have been burned by a bad migration will push on this. Two
honest answers worth having ready: it never guesses at a close-but-not-exact
column - it suggests and waits for you to confirm; and the preview is the
last step before anything is created, so a wrong mapping costs you a click,
not a cleanup.*

### 6. Calendar, tasks, and a booking page they can use today (4 min)

**Show:** create a task, then open the public booking page for the demo
org and book a slot as if you were their customer.

**Talk track:** "Your team's tasks and calendar live right next to the
leads they belong to. And this booking link - you could send this to your
own customers or referral sources starting today, no separate scheduling
tool needed."

### 7. Marketing, built in, not five extra subscriptions (5 min)

**Show:** a simple intake form, a landing page, and one email sent to a
subscriber group.

**Talk track:** "This is your form builder, your landing pages, and your
email sends, all pulling from the same contacts you just saw - not a
separate tool you have to keep in sync by hand."

**Why it matters to them:** consolidation. Fewer logins, fewer monthly
bills, one source of truth.

*If short on time, this is the first section to cut - mention it in one
sentence instead: "There's also a full marketing suite built in, happy to
show that on a follow-up call."*

### 8. Know your numbers (4 min)

**Show:** the pipeline analytics dashboard and one expense or mileage
report. Change the date range while they watch.

**Talk track:** "You can see conversion rate by stage, where things stall,
and if your team tracks expenses or mileage, that rolls straight into a
report - no separate spreadsheet at tax time. Any of these you can narrow to
last month, last quarter, whatever period you're reviewing."

**Why it matters to them:** this is the "prove the ROI" moment - point
directly back to the pain point they named in discovery.

### 9. Build the report we didn't think of (5 min)

This is the strongest section in the demo for anyone who has ever waited on
a vendor for a report. Do not rush it.

**Show:** build one chart live, start to finish. Ask them first: "What's a
number you wish you could see every Monday morning?" Then build *that*, not
a rehearsed example.

Pick the data (leads, referrals, contacts, companies, or one of the custom
modules they built), pick what to measure (a count, or the total, average,
smallest or largest of any number field), pick how to group it (by any
field, by owner, or by day, week or month), and pick how to draw it - bar,
line, pie, table, or a single big number. Save it with a name.

**Talk track:** "That report didn't exist sixty seconds ago, and nobody at
our company had to build it. Whatever you track - including the custom
fields you added yourself earlier - you can measure and chart it. If your
question changes next quarter, you change the report yourself."

**Then show:** put two or three saved charts into one dashboard, give it a
name, drag the tiles into the order you want, and set the date range once at
the top so every chart on the page moves together.

**Talk track:** "Group the ones you look at together into a single page -
your Monday morning review, or the board pack. One date filter at the top
drives all of them, and you drag them into whatever order tells the story."

**Why it matters to them:** this closes the loop opened in Part 1. There we
promised they could add their own fields without waiting on us; here we
prove the reporting keeps up with them. For anyone escaping a rigid legacy
system, this is often the moment they stop evaluating and start planning.

*Tie it to permissions if you covered Part 3: building and editing these is
limited to owners and admissions managers - everyone else can view and run
them, but can't change the numbers the leadership team relies on.*

*If short on time, still build one chart - even ninety seconds of it beats
describing it. Cut the dashboard-grouping half instead.*

### 10. Compliance and trust, if it applies (2 min)

**Show:** the compliance settings screen.

**Talk track:** "If you're handling protected health information, there's a
dedicated compliance mode and we'll sign a business associate agreement with
you directly."

*Only include this section for healthcare or regulated prospects. Skip
entirely otherwise.*

### 11. Simple, fair pricing (2 min)

**Show:** the plans page.

**Talk track:** "Pricing is per seat, so you only pay for the people
actually using it, and adding someone new takes thirty seconds, no
separate contract."

## Closing (5 min)

1. Recap their pain point and the two or three moments that answered it
   directly, in their words: "You said the spreadsheet was losing leads
   between handoffs - that pipeline board with instant custom fields solves
   that day one."
2. Ask directly: "Does this look like something that would work for your
   team?"
3. Propose the next step by name and date, not vaguely: a free trial start
   date, a proposal with pricing for their team size, or a follow-up call
   with the rest of their team.
4. Confirm who else needs to be looped in before a decision gets made.

## Right after the call

- Send a recap email same day: their stated pain point, the two or three
  features that answered it, and the agreed next step with a date.
- Log the call notes and next step wherever the team tracks pipeline, so
  nothing depends on memory.
- If a next step was promised (trial access, a proposal, a follow-up
  invite), send it within one business day - the fastest way to lose a deal
  after a great demo is a slow follow-up.

## Quick do's and don'ts

- Do mirror their vocabulary - if they say "referral," don't say "record."
- Do let them drive occasionally: "What would you want to see next?"
- Do not open on an empty account.
- Do not explain how anything is built - explain what it does for them.
- Do not go over the agreed time. A tight, on-time 30 minutes beats a
  rambling 45.
